# Axioms: Your Analytical Frameworks

> This file is your intellectual operating system. Claude reads it at the start of every session and uses it as the default lens for reasoning with you. It lives in your working folder so both you and Claude can edit it.

---

## What axioms are

An axiom is a framework you think through, not a conclusion you've reached. It tells Claude: "When analysing anything, consider this lens."

Good axioms are:

- **Recurring.** You keep coming back to this idea across different topics. It's not a one-off insight. It's a pattern in how you reason.
- **Connective.** It bridges domains. The best axioms link fields that don't usually talk to each other.
- **Actionable.** It changes what Claude looks for. "Innovation matters" is not an axiom. "Innovation is cumulative recombination, not sudden invention; look for combinatorial potential and network effects before evaluating any new technology" is.

Axioms are not predictions, positions, or conclusions. Those belong in your brain as ingested material. Axioms are the lenses you use to *evaluate* predictions, positions, and conclusions.

---

## How axioms get built

There are two paths, and most people use both:

### Top-down: You declare your frameworks

You already know some of the lenses you apply. Name them. Claude will create initial entries here based on your description and general knowledge. As you ingest material that deepens these frameworks, Claude refines the entries.

Example: "I think about everything through network effects." Claude writes an initial axiom entry. Later you ingest a book on platform economics, and Claude proposes sharpening the entry based on what you highlighted.

### Bottom-up: Patterns emerge from your material

As you ingest sources, Claude notices recurring themes across domains. When a pattern appears in three or more sources, Claude proposes a new axiom: "Your material keeps returning to energy constraints as a limiting factor across AI, infrastructure, and economics. Want me to add an Energy as Constraint axiom?"

You approve, refine, or reject. Claude never adds axioms without your explicit agreement.

---

## How axioms relate to your brain

Your brain (brain.db) holds the depth: the source material, chunks, embeddings, and graph connections. Your axioms hold the *instructions*: which lenses to apply and when.

When Claude encounters a substantive question:
1. It reads your axioms (this file) to know which frameworks matter to you.
2. It searches your brain for relevant depth on those frameworks.
3. It synthesises: your lens applied to your knowledge, grounded in your sources.

The axiom says "look for network effects." The brain contains the detailed material on how network effects work. They serve different purposes and neither replaces the other.
